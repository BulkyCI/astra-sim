The pre-registered expectation that no-CC would collapse harder at 4:1
than at 1:1 (+55 %) was wrong: the rise is +36 %. The reason is in the
topology. At 1:1 the 62 remote sources reach the victim's leaf over eight
spines and can offer 63 times the victim link's rate, so trimmed headers
alone overflow the trimmed-traffic class; at 4:1 they reach it over two
spines, so the offered load at the victim link is at most two to one, the
header flood is milder, and the burst drains in 734 ms instead of 846 ms.
The direction of the claim holds on both fabrics: trimming without a
controller is the fastest configuration on the symmetric collective and
the slowest under the incast, FORGIVE keeps its lead in both.

## 4. Column semantics, for anyone recomputing

`flow_events.csv`: `delivered_bytes` is what the receiver accepted;
`forgiven_bytes` the ranges it forgave; `retransmitted_bytes` the bytes
re-sent; `trimmed_payload_bytes` counts trimmed bytes that were repaired,
not the forgiven ones (the fabric's total trims are `trim_ftd_admission`
in `ns3/transport_summary.csv`, and `trim_forgiven` the forgiven share).
"Resent" in the paper is retransmitted bytes over all bytes the job
sends; "Lost" is over data-parallel bytes.
